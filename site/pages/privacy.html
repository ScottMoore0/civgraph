Privacy

What Civgraph collects about the people who read it, the people who contribute to it and the people named in its records, and how to ask us to correct or remove something.

Summary

  • Civgraph has no advertising, no tracking cookies and no third-party analytics.
  • The map page sends anonymous speed measurements to our own server. They carry the page address and whether you are on a phone or a computer, not your IP address or any identifier.
  • Your browser remembers a few settings, such as light or dark mode, on your own device. They are never sent to us.
  • Some features fetch from other services: the background map comes from OpenStreetMap, and the place search in the map catalogue asks a geocoding service. Those services see your IP address.
  • Civgraph publishes the names, parties, results and public records of election candidates and office-holders, because they are part of the public record. If something about you is wrong, or you think it should not be here, email us.

When you read the site

Speed measurements on the map

The map page at /maps/ measures how quickly it loads and responds, using the open-source web-vitals library served from civgraph.net. It reports five standard measures (largest contentful paint, interaction to next paint, cumulative layout shift, first contentful paint and time to first byte). Each report holds:

  • the measure, its value and its rating (good, needs improvement or poor);
  • the page path, with any query string and # part removed before it is sent and again when it arrives;
  • "mobile" or "desktop", worked out in your browser. Your full browser string is not sent;
  • a random number that the library makes for each page view, so one view's repeat reports can be counted once. It is not stored on your device and does not link one visit to another.

When the map's election data fails to load, the page also reports the page path, the error message and which election layer failed, so we can fix it.

These reports go to /_api/rum on civgraph.net. Our code writes each one as a single log line and does not record your IP address with it. They are not saved to a database. No report is sent when the site runs on a developer's own computer.

OWNER: the code only writes to the Cloudflare Functions log. Confirm whether Workers Logs, Logpush or any other log storage is switched on for the civgraph project in the Cloudflare dashboard, and for how long it keeps logs, so this page can state a retention period.

Hosting and server logs

Civgraph is hosted by Cloudflare: the pages, the record database and the map files at data.civgraph.net all run on Cloudflare's network. As with any website, Cloudflare receives your IP address, browser string, the address you asked for and the time, so that it can deliver the page and protect the site from abuse. Cloudflare processes this for us under its own terms and privacy policy.

The site's own code does not log IP addresses and adds no analytics script.

OWNER: features switched on in the Cloudflare dashboard are not visible in the code. Confirm whether Cloudflare Web Analytics, Bot Fight Mode or other bot management (which can set a __cf_bm cookie) are on, and how long Cloudflare keeps request logs on your plan.

What your browser stores

Civgraph sets no cookies for readers. It keeps a few settings in your browser's local storage, which stays on your device and is never sent to us:

  • light or dark theme (theme);
  • the language preview, if you turn it on (civgraph.langPreview, civgraph.lang);
  • map and catalogue preferences, such as the background map you chose, layer order, panel layout, text size, catalogue view and whether you have closed a hint (keys beginning civgraph. or civgraph:, and basemapUserChoice).

Browse remembers how far down a page you had scrolled, in session storage, which your browser clears when you close the tab. A service worker keeps copies of the site's pages and files on your device so that it loads faster and works offline.

You can clear all of these at any time in your browser's settings for civgraph.net.

Other services your browser contacts

Some features load material directly from other organisations. When they do, your browser sends that organisation your IP address and browser string, as it would for any website. Civgraph sends them nothing else. Our pages tell browsers to send only "civgraph.net" as the referring site, not the full page address.

When you email us

Corrections, map submissions and requests to become a contributor come to scott@civgraph.net. We receive your email address, any name you give and what you write. We use them to reply, to check what you tell us, and, if you ask us to, to credit you by name for a correction or a map. We do not use them for anything else, and we do not add you to a mailing list.

OWNER: say which provider hosts the civgraph.net mailbox, and how long you keep correspondence (for example, "as long as it is needed to check and credit the correction, then deleted within N years").

If you are a contributor

A small number of approved contributors can propose changes on the site. Signing in uses Cloudflare Access with GitHub as the login provider. GitHub tells Cloudflare your primary verified email address. Cloudflare then sets a sign-in cookie (CF_Authorization) for the contributor tools, and the site checks your address against the list of approved contributors.

When you submit a proposed change, the site stores it in a review queue on Cloudflare together with your email address and your browser string, so that we know who proposed what and can follow up. Nothing you submit appears on the site until an administrator approves it. If you ask, approved contributions are credited to you by name.

OWNER: queue entries have no expiry in the code, so they are kept indefinitely. Decide a retention period (for example, delete the email address and browser string N months after a submission is decided) and state it here.

People named in the records

Civgraph is a reference work on Irish civic life. It publishes, for people who have stood for election or held public office:

  • names, and the dates and places of the elections they stood in;
  • their party or description on the ballot, their votes and whether they were elected;
  • offices they held, and how members voted in parliamentary divisions;
  • entries in the registers of members' interests that the parliaments themselves publish;
  • links to Wikidata, Wikipedia and other public records about them.

All of it comes from records already made public by the people concerned or by public bodies: official election results, gazettes, parliamentary records and registers, and historical archives. Each record shows where it came from.

We do not publish home addresses. Where a source such as a nomination paper gives an address, we record at most the small statistical area it lies in, never the address itself.

OWNER: confirm the address rule as stated (your rule: smallest census unit, raw addresses private) and that nominators, proposers and agents are not published today.

Why we may publish it

Publishing these records is in the public interest. Elections and public office are public acts, and the record of who stood, who won and how they voted is how citizens, researchers and journalists hold power to account and understand history. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest, and the public's, in keeping and publishing that record. A person's party is a political opinion, which data protection law treats with extra care; we publish it only where the person made it public themselves by standing under that label, or where it is in an archival or historical record. Much of the material is historical and is kept as an archive in the public interest.

We do not contact everyone named in the records to tell them, because there are tens of thousands of them, many from long ago. This page is how we tell them instead.

OWNER: this follows UK GDPR and GDPR Article 6(1)(f) (legitimate interests), Article 9(2)(e) (made manifestly public by the person) and the archiving and historical research provisions (Article 89, and in the UK the Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1 and Schedule 2 Part 6), and relies on Article 14(5)(b) for not notifying each person. Take advice before approval. Decide also whether to write a short legitimate interests assessment.

Your rights, and how to ask for a correction or removal

You can ask us to show you what we hold about you, to correct it, or to stop publishing it. Email scott@civgraph.net with a link to the page, or the name of the record, and say what you want us to do.

  • Corrections. If a record is wrong, we will check it against the source and fix it. A link or reference to the right figure helps.
  • Removal or objection. We will consider every request. We weigh it against the public interest in a complete record of elections and public office, and we may keep the core result (that a person stood, where, when and how many votes they received) while removing anything else that is not needed.
  • Contributors and correspondents can ask us to delete their email address and submissions at any time, unless we still need them to deal with something they reported.

We will reply within one month. If you are unhappy with our answer you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK or the Data Protection Commission in Ireland.
