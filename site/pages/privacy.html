Privacy

What Civgraph collects about the people who read it, the people who contribute to it and the people named in its records, which other services your browser contacts, and how to ask us to correct or remove something.

Summary

  • Civgraph shows no advertising and sets no tracking or advertising cookies. The site's own code contains no analytics script.
  • Your browser keeps a few settings, such as light or dark mode and how you arranged the map, on your own device. They are not sent to us.
  • The map page sends anonymous speed measurements to our own server. They carry the page path and whether you are on a phone or a computer, not your IP address or any identifier.
  • Some features load material from other services. The background map comes from OpenFreeMap, and the place search in the map catalogue asks a geocoding service. Those services see your IP address.
  • Civgraph publishes the names, parties, results and public records of election candidates and office-holders, taken from public records. It does not publish home addresses.
  • To ask for a correction or removal, email Scott@civgraph.net.

Hosting

Civgraph runs on Cloudflare. Cloudflare serves the pages, runs the code that answers record pages and the API, holds the records database, and serves the map files and downloads at data.civgraph.net. As with any website, Cloudflare receives your IP address, your browser's description of itself, the address you asked for and the time, so that it can deliver the page and protect the site from abuse. It handles this under its own privacy policy.

The site's own code does not record IP addresses and includes no analytics script, such as Cloudflare Web Analytics.

Speed measurements on the map

The map pages measure how quickly they load and respond, using the open-source web-vitals library served from civgraph.net. They report five standard measures: largest contentful paint, interaction to next paint, cumulative layout shift, first contentful paint and time to first byte. Each report holds:

  • the measure, its value and its rating (good, needs improvement or poor);
  • the page path, without any query string or # part (the server removes them again when the report arrives);
  • "mobile" or "desktop", worked out in your browser. Your full browser string is not sent;
  • a random number the library makes for each page view, so that repeat reports from one view can be counted once. It is not stored on your device and does not link one visit to another.

When the map's election data fails to load, the page also reports the page path, the error and which election layer failed, so that we can fix it.

The reports go to /_api/rum on civgraph.net. Our code writes each one as a single log line, with no IP address, and does not save it to a database. No report is sent when the site runs on a developer's own computer.

When you email us

Corrections, map submissions and requests to become a contributor come to Scott@civgraph.net. We receive your email address, any name you give and what you write. We use them to reply, to check what you tell us and, if you ask us to, to credit you by name for a correction or a map. We do not use them for anything else, and we do not add you to a mailing list.

If you are a contributor

A small number of approved contributors can propose changes on the site. The contributor sign-in link appears in Browse only when you open a page with ?contribute in its address, and is then remembered in that browser (see What your browser stores). In the map catalogue, the "Suggest an edit" item in a map's menu also leads to the sign-in.

Signing in uses Cloudflare Access, with GitHub as the login provider. GitHub tells Cloudflare your primary verified email address. Cloudflare then sets its own sign-in cookie (CF_Authorization) for the contributor tools, and the site checks your address against the list of approved contributors.

When you submit a proposed change, the site stores it in a review queue on Cloudflare together with your email address and your browser's description of itself, so that we know who proposed what and can follow up. Nothing you submit appears on the site until an administrator approves it. If you ask, approved contributions are credited to you by name.

People named in the records

Civgraph is a reference work on Irish civic life. For people who have stood for election or held public office, it publishes:

  • names, and the dates and places of the elections they stood in;
  • their party or description on the ballot, their votes and whether they were elected;
  • offices they held, and how members voted in parliamentary divisions;
  • entries in the registers of members' interests, as the parliaments themselves publish them;
  • links to Wikidata, Wikipedia and other public records about them.

All of it comes from records already made public by the people concerned or by public bodies: official election results, gazettes, parliamentary records and registers, and historical archives. Each record shows where it came from.

Civgraph does not publish home addresses. The records have no address field. Where a source, such as a nomination paper, gives an address, Civgraph records at most the small statistical area it lies in (a census small area or output area), never the address itself.

Why we may publish it

Elections and public office are public acts. The record of who stood, who won and how they voted is how citizens, researchers and journalists hold power to account and understand history, and much of Civgraph's material is historical, kept as an archive in the public interest. A person's party is a political opinion, which data protection law treats with extra care. Civgraph publishes it only where the person made it public by standing under that label, or where it is in an archival or historical record.

We do not write to everyone named in the records, because there are tens of thousands of them, many from long ago. This page is how we tell them instead.

How to ask for a correction or removal

Email Scott@civgraph.net with a link to the page, or the name of the record, and say what you would like us to do. You can ask us what we hold about you, to correct it, or to stop publishing it.

  • Corrections. If a record is wrong, we will check it against the source and fix it. A link or reference to the right figure helps.
  • Removal or objection. We will consider every request, and weigh it against the public interest in a complete record of elections and public office.
  • Contributors and correspondents can ask us to delete their email address and submissions.

If you are unhappy with our answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK or the Data Protection Commission in Ireland.
