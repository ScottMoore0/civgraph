Licence

What you may do with Civgraph's records, maps and downloads, the licence of each source they come from, and how to credit both.

In short

  • Civgraph's own compiled data and original text are released under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0). You may reuse them for any purpose, including commercially, as long as you credit Civgraph.
  • Much of what Civgraph shows comes from other bodies. That material keeps its own licence, listed below, and each asks for its own credit. Crediting Civgraph alone is not enough.
  • Every record says where it came from, and a map or record that names a licence of its own is under that licence.

Civgraph's own work: CC BY 4.0

Civgraph's own work is what it compiles and writes: the election results it has compiled and checked, the links it has made between people, places, offices and events, the counts it derives (such as Words in parliament), the catalogue descriptions and other original text on the site, and the downloads that package them. Unless a record says otherwise, this work is released under CC BY 4.0.

You may copy it, publish it, adapt it and use it commercially, as long as you:

  • credit Civgraph, and link to the record or to civgraph.net;
  • say if you changed it;
  • also follow the licence of the original source, below.

Where a record says otherwise

A map or record that names its own licence is under that licence, whatever this page says in general.

  • Contributed digitisations. Maps digitised by volunteers are credited to them by name on each map. Some, such as Poor Law Unions, 1911, record that they are released under CC BY-SA 4.0, and that licence applies to them.
  • Maps and records from another body. A boundary drawn by Ordnance Survey of Northern Ireland, Tailte Éireann or a statistics office, or a record taken from a parliament or an archive, stays under that body's licence. The record names the body.

Scans of old works

Civgraph's scans of books, debates and Acts whose copyright has expired are offered without any new copyright claim. Use them as you would the originals.

Background maps

The background map on /maps/ is not Civgraph's. By default it is drawn by OpenFreeMap from OpenMapTiles and OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence). If OpenFreeMap cannot be reached, OpenStreetMap's own map is shown instead. Other background maps you can choose credit their own providers. The map always shows the credit for the background in use: keep it on any screenshot.

The software

The code that runs Civgraph is released under the MIT licence. The MIT licence covers the software only. It does not relicense any of the data. The browser code includes third-party open-source packages under their own licences, whose notices ship with it.

How to credit

Give both Civgraph and the original source. For example:

Source: Civgraph (https://civgraph.net), CC BY 4.0, compiled from the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland's result sheets (Open Government Licence v3.0).

For a map:

Boundaries: Poor Law Unions, 1911, digitised by Scott Moore, Phelim Birch and Paddy Matthews for Civgraph (CC BY-SA 4.0). Background map: OpenFreeMap, © OpenMapTiles, data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

For academic citations, see How to cite.

Personal data

Civgraph's records name candidates and office-holders. A licence covers copyright and database rights, not data protection. If you reuse records about living people, data protection law applies to what you do with them. See Privacy.

If you hold rights in something here

If you think something on Civgraph is published against its licence, or wrongly credited, email Scott@civgraph.net with a link to it. We will look into it promptly, and take it down while we check if that is needed.
