Licence

What you may do with Civgraph's maps, records and downloads, and how to credit them and the sources they come from.

Draft for approval, 11 Nov 2026. Nothing on this page is final. Notes marked OWNER are points the drafter could not verify, or decisions only the owner can make. They are removed, with this banner and the decision box below, when the page is approved.

OWNER DECISION: which licence covers Civgraph's own data?

The site says different things today:

  • Every page footer says "© Civgraph", with no licence.
  • The support box on every page says "I release all my maps under an open source license (Creative Commons BY-SA 4.0)".
  • About lists each source's licence and says the site is "released under open licences", but names none for Civgraph's own work.
  • The repository's LICENSE is MIT (© 2026 Scott Moore), and NOTICE says it covers the software only, not the data.
  • In the map catalogue, 36 contributed digitisations record CC BY-SA 4.0; 874 of the 1,118 catalogue entries record no licence at all.
  • The downloads manifest says "See https://civgraph.net/pages/about.html for the licence of each source", and the three own-work entries in the source ledger name no licence.

Choose one:

  1. CC BY-SA 4.0 for everything Civgraph makes (digitised boundaries, compiled records, derived counts, the downloads). This matches the support box and the 36 layers, and fits the ElectionsNI material (CC BY-SA 4.0 and ODbL) the STV animation derives from. Share-alike can put off reusers who want to combine it with material under other terms.
  2. CC BY 4.0 for everything Civgraph makes. The easiest to reuse, and it fits the OGL and CC BY sources. The contributed maps already released as CC BY-SA would stay CC BY-SA unless their contributors agree to the change.
  3. A split: ODbL for the records database and downloads (a licence written for databases), and CC BY-SA 4.0 for digitised maps.

Then confirm the contributors (Phelim Birch, Paddy Matthews and others) agree to it for their digitisations. Change the footer to "© Civgraph contributors · Licence · Privacy", reword the support box to match, and put the licence in the downloads manifest and the own-work ledger entries.

This draft is written as option 1, because it is what the site already says most often. Text that depends on the choice is marked.

In short

  • You may reuse Civgraph's maps, records and downloads, including commercially, as long as you credit Civgraph and the original source, and share what you make from them under the same terms. OWNER: depends on the decision above.
  • Much of what Civgraph shows comes from other bodies, under their own licences. Those licences still apply, and each asks for its own credit. Crediting Civgraph alone is not enough.
  • Every record on the site says where it came from. Credit that source.

Civgraph's own data

Civgraph's own work is the boundaries its volunteers have digitised, the election results it has compiled and checked, the links it has made between people, places and events, the counts it derives (such as Words in parliament), and the downloads that package them. Unless a record says otherwise, this work is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence (CC BY-SA 4.0). OWNER: depends on the decision above.

That means you may copy it, publish it, change it and use it commercially, as long as you:

  • credit Civgraph and its contributors, and link to the record or to civgraph.net;
  • say if you changed it;
  • release what you make from it under the same licence;
  • also follow the licence of the original source (below).

Contributed maps are credited to the people who digitised them, by name, on each map. The rights of the original map are recorded separately from the rights in the digitisation.

The software

The code that runs Civgraph is released under the MIT licence. The MIT licence covers the software only. It does not relicense any of the data. The browser bundle includes third-party open-source packages under the MIT, BSD-3-Clause and Apache-2.0 licences, whose notices ship with it. Scans of old works

Civgraph's scans of books, debates and Acts whose copyright has expired are offered without any new copyright claim. Use them as you would the originals.

How to credit

Give both Civgraph and the original source. For example:

Source: Civgraph (https://civgraph.net), CC BY-SA 4.0, compiled from the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland result sheets (Open Government Licence v3.0).

For a map:

Boundaries: Poor Law Unions, 1911, digitised by Scott Moore, Phelim Birch and Paddy Matthews for Civgraph (CC BY-SA 4.0). Background map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

For academic citations, see How to cite. OWNER: CC BY-SA in these examples depends on the decision above.

Personal data

Civgraph's records name candidates and office-holders. A licence covers copyright and database rights, not data protection. If you reuse records about living people, data protection law applies to what you do with them. See Privacy.

If you hold rights in something here

If you think something on Civgraph is published against its licence, or wrongly credited, email scott@civgraph.net with a link to it. We will look into it promptly and take it down while we check, if that is needed.
