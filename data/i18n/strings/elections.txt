@elections.methods.shares_title Vote shares ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.shares_link How shares are worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.shares_p1 A party's share of the vote at an election is its candidates' votes added up, divided by the votes of every candidate in the contests that went to a poll, as a percentage. Under the single transferable vote the votes are first preferences. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.shares_p2 A contest returned unopposed counts towards seats and candidates but has no votes, so it adds nothing to either side of the division. Where some contests of an election have no recorded votes, the shares are of the contests that have them, and the page says how many those are. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.shares_p3 A candidate's share in a contest is their votes divided by the contest's valid poll as the source gives it. Where the source gives no valid poll, the candidates' votes added up stand for it, and the contest page says so. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.shares_p4 In the pivot tables, a share of the votes or seats in the column is a cell's votes or seats divided by those of its whole column. The average candidate share is the plain mean of each candidate's share of their own contest's valid poll. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.losers_title Shares when the losing candidates are missing ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.losers_link How these shares are affected ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.losers_p1 Some contests are on file with only the members elected: the source printed no losing candidates, or the sources read so far name only the winners. A contest counts as one of these when it went to a poll and has no more candidates on file than seats. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.losers_p2 Their votes are counted as they are. An election's party votes and shares therefore leave out the losing candidates' votes in those contests, and a contest's own shares are of the elected members' votes alone. No majority is given for such a contest, since the runner-up's votes are not known. Each page with such figures says how many contests are affected. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.change_title Change since the previous election ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.change_link How change is worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.change_p1 The seat change (±) is a party's seats at this election minus its seats at the body's previous general election. The share change (± pts) is the difference between its two vote shares, in percentage points. A vote change is given only when both elections have recorded votes. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.change_p2 Change is shown between general elections only: a by-election is not compared, since the election before it filled every seat. Parties are matched by their names as the party table gives them (see party labels); a party that won seats last time and did not stand this time is listed with its losses. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.change_p3 Compare sets any two elections side by side the same way. Between two bodies, parties are matched by their labels only. Contest by contest, contests are matched by name, and a contest is marked changed when its winning party differs, or, where it elects several members, the party with most seats. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swing_title Swing on the results map ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swing_link How swing is worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swing_p1 The map's change mode shows, area by area, a party's share at this election minus its share at an earlier election of the same body fought on the same boundaries, in percentage points. A party's share in an area is its votes there divided by all the votes there (first preferences under the single transferable vote). ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swing_p2 Parties are matched by their ballot label as printed. An area with no recorded votes at either election is hatched. No change is mapped across a boundary change: the notional results carry votes across one. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.turnout_link How turnout is worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.turnout_p1 A contest's turnout is the figure its source gives. Where the source gives none but gives the total poll and the electorate, turnout is the total poll divided by the electorate, as a percentage, and the contest page says it was worked out. Where the source gives the turnout but not the total poll, the total poll is the electorate times the turnout, rounded to a whole vote. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.turnout_p2 An election's turnout is the median of its contests' turnouts, with the lowest and the highest. Contests with no turnout on file, those returned unopposed among them, are left out. The pivot tables' average turnout is the plain mean of the contests' turnouts, each contest counted once. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.majority_title Majority and quota ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.majority_link How the majority is worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.majority_p1 Under first past the post the majority is the winner's votes minus the runner-up's. It is the source's figure where the source gives one; otherwise it is worked out from the candidates' votes, and the contest page says so. Its percentage is of the valid poll. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.majority_p2 Under the single transferable vote a contest gives its quota instead, as the source states it. Progress to the quota is each candidate's votes at their last count divided by the quota. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.party_labels_title Party labels ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.party_labels_link How party labels are grouped ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.party_labels_p1 Candidates stand under the label printed on the ballot paper, and the records keep that label as printed. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.party_labels_p2 An election's party table groups the labels. Labels that differ only in capital letters, accents, punctuation, '&' for 'and', or a note in brackets count as one party, shown under the first spelling met. At Dáil elections a few older spellings are read as the current name (Sinn Fein as Sinn Féin, Fianna Fail as Fianna Fáil, Labour as Irish Labour, People Before Profit as PBP), and a candidate with no label counts as Independent. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.party_labels_p3 The pivot tables, the share and change maps, the swingometer and the notional results do not group labels: each label as printed is a party of its own there, so one party can appear under several spellings. A candidacy with no label reads No party recorded. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.incumbency_title Incumbency ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.incumbency_link How incumbency is worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.incumbency_p1 Incumbency is worked out, not taken from a source. A candidate counts as an incumbent when the same person, by Civgraph's person link, was elected to the same body at its previous general election or at a by-election since. Someone elected then who left the body before this election still counts. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.incumbency_p2 Incumbency is unknown for a candidacy with no person link, and for every candidacy at the first election of a body that Civgraph holds. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.seat_estimates_title Seat estimates from first preferences ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.seat_estimates_link How seats are estimated ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.seat_estimates_p1 The swingometer and the notional results estimate seats from vote shares alone. A contest with one seat goes to the party with the largest share. In a contest with several seats, each party gets a seat for every whole quota of 100 ÷ (seats + 1) per cent in its share, and any seats left go to the largest remainders. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.seat_estimates_p2 A party's candidates are pooled, so the estimate is of the party's seats, not of who would win them. Under the single transferable vote the real count transfers votes, which often decides the last seats. Transfers are not modelled, so these estimates can differ from the seats actually won, even with no change at all. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swingometer_title The swingometer ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swingometer_link How the swingometer works ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swingometer_p1 The swingometer starts from a base election's votes by contest and party (first preferences under the single transferable vote). Moving a party's slider adds that many points to its share in every contest: a uniform swing. A share cannot fall below zero, and each contest's shares are then scaled to add up to 100. Seats are then estimated as above. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.swingometer_p2 Each party with at least 0.5% of the base election's vote has a slider, from −15 to +15 points. The base seats are the same estimate with no change, so they can differ from the seats actually won. The new share is each party's share of the whole vote with its change applied, scaled with the others to add up to 100. No poll is used. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.notional_title Notional results ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.notional_link How notional results are worked out ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.notional_p1 A notional result estimates how an election would have gone on new boundaries. Each old constituency's votes are shared out among the new constituencies that take in part of it, by one of two weights: the share of its 2021 usual residents living in each (population-weighted, with each 2021 Data Zone counted whole in the old and new constituency that hold its central point), or the share of its land area (area-weighted). ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.notional_p2 A new constituency's notional votes are the sum, over the old constituencies, of each one's votes times its weight. Each new constituency has as many seats as the base election's contests had on average, estimated from its notional shares as above. The projection takes the shares you type, works out each party's change from its notional share, and applies that change as a uniform swing. These are Civgraph's estimates, not official notional results. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.checked_title Data checked dates ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.checked_link What the checked date means ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.checked_p1 Where Civgraph fetched a source and compared it with a contest figure by figure, the source carries the day of that comparison. A contest page gives, beside its figures, the latest such day among the sources that agreed with every figure compared, and each source in its list gives its own day. ¦ ¦
@elections.methods.checked_p2 A source that was compared but disagreed somewhere carries its day too, with what differed. Sources checked another way, such as a printed volume read page by page, carry no day. ¦ ¦

How it is worked out ¦ ¦
Every party keeps exactly the votes it won: no tactical voting, no change of candidates, the same constituencies. ¦ ¦
Only first preferences are used: the transfers of an STV count are left out. ¦ ¦
D'Hondt gives each seat in turn to the list with the most votes divided by 1, 2, 3 and so on (one more than the seats it holds). ¦ ¦
Sainte-Laguë does the same with the divisors 1, 3, 5 and so on, which is kinder to smaller lists. ¦ ¦
Pure PR shares out all the seats as one area: each list gets a seat for every full quota of votes (all the votes divided by the seats), and the seats left go to the largest remainders. ¦ ¦
First past the post gives each constituency to the candidate with the most first preferences. ¦ ¦
A party is the label on the ballot paper as recorded. A candidate with no party, or with a label beginning "Independent", stands alone and can win one seat at most. ¦ ¦
